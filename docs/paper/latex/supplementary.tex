\section{The frequency-aware margin factor $m_c$: exact instantiation}\label{app:mc}

This section gives the complete instantiation of the class-specific threshold scaling factor $m_c$ referenced in Section~3.3.2 of the main text (Eq.~(3) there): the concrete functional form of $f(\cdot)$, its default sensitivity parameters, the calibration rule that fixes the base threshold $\tau$, and the pre-registered ablation hooks.

\subsection{Functional form}

With $\pi_c$ the seed prior of class $c$ (the fraction of verified seed anchors labeled $c$), $\pi_{\max} = \max_{c'} \pi_{c'}$, an exponent $\alpha \geq 0$, and a floor ratio $\rho \in (0,1]$, the scaling factor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mc-supp}
m_c \;=\; f\!\left(\pi_c / \pi_{\max}\right) \;=\; \max\!\left(\rho,\; \left(\pi_c / \pi_{\max}\right)^{\alpha}\right),
\qquad \tau_c \;=\; \tau \cdot m_c .
\end{equation}
Rare classes ($\pi_c \ll \pi_{\max}$) receive $m_c \leq 1$, i.e.\ a lowered admission bar that counters long-tail suppression; majority classes receive $m_c \approx 1$; and the floor $\rho$ prevents the bar from collapsing on extreme tails. Setting $\alpha = 0$ disables the mechanism ($m_c \equiv 1$ for all classes), which is the ablated fallback configuration.

\subsection{Default values and reference implementation}

The protocol defaults, used unchanged in every reported arm (E4, E7, E7b, E9 series), are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Parameter & Default & Role \\
\midrule
Exponent $\alpha$ & $1.0$ & frequency-aware scaling strength ($0$ = off) \\
Floor ratio $\rho$ & $0.5$ & minimum scaling factor (admission bar never below $\tau/2$) \\
Base threshold $\tau$ & calibrated per run & see Section~\ref{app:mc-calib} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The reference implementation is the function \texttt{frequency\_aware\_thresholds} in \texttt{psd/training/jia\_prototype.py} of the released repository (signature \texttt{(tau, label\_priors, alpha=1.0, floor\_ratio=0.5)}), consumed per round by the self-training loop in \texttt{psd/training/tcl\_selftrain.py}; the entering scripts (\texttt{scripts/run\_p04\_tcl.py}, \texttt{scripts/run\_p07\_endtoend\_ak.py}, \texttt{scripts/run\_r16\_ntu\_pseudo\_10seed.py} and the other E9-series entry points) pin \texttt{alpha=1.0} and never override \texttt{floor\_ratio}. The seed prior $\pi_c$ is computed from the anchor-side labels only (GT-independent), and is re-estimated each round from the current labeled set (verified seeds $\cup$ confidence-filtered pool), so $m_c$ tracks the labeling state exactly as the prototype re-estimation of Eq.~(1) of the main text does.

\subsection{Base-threshold calibration}\label{app:mc-calib}

The base threshold $\tau$ is fixed once by quantile targeting: $\tau$ is the $(1 - \text{target-coverage})$ quantile of the effective scores $\kappa(u)/m_{\hat{y}_u}$ over the admissible proposal universe at the first round, with target coverage $0.35$ (pre-registered). The same $\tau$ is reused in every later round to prevent drift-based selection; only $m_c$ updates as the labeled set grows. No ground-truth labels enter this calibration.

\subsection{Ablation hooks}

Two pre-registered ablation hooks isolate the factor's contribution, both released in \texttt{configs/p04\_tcl.yaml} and reported in the ablation reports:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\alpha$ ablation.} $\alpha = 0$ (uniform thresholds; \texttt{freq\_alpha\_ablation} in the config) against the default $\alpha = 1$ (\texttt{freq\_alpha\_default}), with the intermediate value $\alpha = 0.5$ included in the sweep grid.
\item \textbf{Coverage-target sweep.} The quantile target $\in \{0.25, 0.35, 0.50\}$, which moves the operating point along the precision--coverage curve while holding $f(\cdot)$ fixed.
\end{itemize}
The floor ratio $\rho$ is held at $0.5$ throughout; it is a safety parameter rather than a tuned one, and sweeping it is left as an explicitly unclaimed direction (main text, Section~5, Sensitivity).

\section{Pre-registered control protocols for the transition-cost and attribution claims}\label{app:trans}

The main text claims a $\geq 3\times$ lower retraining cost for absorbing a taxonomy transition on the synthetic tier (E6) and pre-registers controls to attribute that saving to the semantic layer rather than to freezing alone (main text, Section~4.4, Attribution controls). This section documents the registered protocols and their outcomes in full. Throughout, the decision vocabulary is the registered one: \textbf{CONFIRMS} (cost ratio $\geq 3\times$ and accuracy inside the $\pm 2.3$\,pp noise band), \textbf{PARTIAL} (cost ratio $\geq 3\times$ but accuracy out of band), \textbf{FAILS} (cost ratio $< 3\times$); verdicts are reported regardless of direction.

\subsection{PSD-NTU-TRANS-001: MLP-scale two-arm replication on NTU60}\label{app:trans001}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}
Protocol ID PSD-NTU-TRANS-001, frozen v1.0 on 2026-09-07, before any run (the map-construction smoke test touches no training data and precedes the freeze). Pre-registration text: \texttt{docs/paper/ntu-transition-preregistration.md}; driver: \texttt{scripts/run\_r22\_ntu\_transition.py}; evidence: \texttt{reports/r22-ntu-transition-2026-09-07.json}.

\paragraph{Purpose.}
E6's $\geq 3\times$ transition-cost saving was established on the synthetic tier only, and the review identified the coupled baseline as the most expensive alternative. This protocol replicates the E6 two-arm design on a real benchmark.

\paragraph{Data (frozen).}
NTU RGB+D 60 cross-subject, HRNet 2D joints (\texttt{data/pyskl/ntu60\_hrnet.pkl}; 56{,}578 clips; 40{,}091 train / 16{,}487 val; $\approx$666 train clips per class), the same source as the E9b replication.

\paragraph{Scenario (frozen).}
$\mathcal{Y}$ = the 60 official NTU60 classes; $\mathcal{Y}'$ = 49 classes produced by ten frozen same-family merge operations with a coarse ``daily-report'' semantics (Table~\ref{tab:mergemap}); the remaining 50 classes pass through unchanged. The merge map is hard-coded in \texttt{build\_y\_to\_yp\_map\_ntu()} and was frozen before any run.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{The ten frozen merge operations of PSD-NTU-TRANS-001/002 ($\mathcal{Y}$: 60 classes $\to$ $\mathcal{Y}'$: 49 classes).}\label{tab:mergemap}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\# & Merged source classes & $\mathcal{Y}'$ class \\
\midrule
1 & drink water; eat meal/snack & consume \\

10 & walking towards; walking apart from each other & bidirectional walk \\
\bottomrule

\paragraph{Arms (frozen; same-architecture control).}
Both arms share one backbone architecture (joint-level MLP: flattened joint coordinates $\to$ 512 $\to$ 256 penultimate; the E9b/c caliber), the same data, the same $\mathcal{Y}'$-mapped labels, the same 8:2 split, and the same 80-epoch budget:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm D (decoupled).} The MLP pretext is trained once, self-supervised, on $\mathcal{Y}$-era data (80 epochs, no labels); the penultimate is frozen and only a linear head $\Omega$ is retrained on $\mathcal{Y}'$-mapped labels. At the transition, only $\Omega$ is retrained.
\item \textbf{Arm C (coupled).} The identical architecture is trained end-to-end, supervised, from scratch on $\mathcal{Y}'$-mapped labels (pretext and head jointly, 80 epochs)---a minimal realization of the coupled alternative.
\end{itemize}
Annotation units are isomorphic across arms (arm C's backpropagation labels are arm D's head labels), so any cost difference is computational, not labeling-driven---the same construction as E6.

\paragraph{Budgets and seeds (frozen).}
Two budget tiers: \emph{full} (40{,}091 $\mathcal{Y}'$-mapped training labels) and \emph{10\%} (4{,}009 clips, stratified, seed-42 fixed subset, the E9 convention). Seeds 42/43/44 (the E6 precedent: the primary endpoint is a wall-clock ratio, so the E9-series ten-seed convention does not apply; seeds enter only the accuracy band, and this deviation is disclosed in the protocol).

\paragraph{Decision rule (frozen).}
\textbf{CONFIRMS}: median wall-clock ratio $D/C \geq 3\times$ \emph{and} $|\mathrm{acc}_D - \mathrm{acc}_C| < 2.3$\,pp; \textbf{PARTIAL}: ratio $\geq 3\times$ but accuracy out of band; \textbf{FAILS}: ratio $< 3\times$ (the $\geq 3\times$ claim then remains scoped to the synthetic tier). A pre-registered asymmetric expectation was recorded to prevent post-hoc interpretation: arm C may dominate at full budget (the L10 gradient) while arm D may dominate at 10\% labels; both tiers are reported as observed.

\paragraph{Disclosures (frozen).}
The backbone caliber is the joint-level MLP, not the ST-GCN of the equivalence verification---a deliberately conservative choice, since an ST-GCN coupled arm would retrain in days and inflate the ratio. Arm C is a minimal coupled pipeline, not a published method; no inter-method SOTA comparison is made. The $\mathcal{Y}'$ map is author-designed. Wall-clock is measured as in E6 (the \texttt{trainer.fit()} envelope). The protocol pinned architecture, epochs, budgets, and seeds but \emph{not} solver families: the D head uses the E9-series CPU logistic-regression convention while C trains by GPU SGD; no post-hoc matched-solver re-run is claimed under this protocol.

\paragraph{Results (post-run, verbatim from the evidence file).}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccccl}
\toprule
Budget & D head (med.) & C full (med.) & $C/D$ & Acc D & Acc C & Gap & Verdict \\
\midrule
full & 465.9\,s & 34.9\,s & 0.07$\times$ & 66.13\% & 62.20\% & $+3.93$\,pp & FAILS \\
10\% & 8.6\,s & 3.8\,s & 0.44$\times$ & 62.27\% & 50.97\% & $+11.30$\,pp & FAILS \\

Both cost endpoints \textbf{FAIL} (factor $< 3\times$): at MLP scale the entire coupled pipeline retrains in $\approx$35\,s, so there is no wall-clock cost for decoupling to save. The pre-registered accuracy expectation was \emph{not} confirmed as stated: the decoupled arm was more accurate at every budget ($+3.93$\,pp full, $+11.30$\,pp at 10\%, both outside the $\pm 2.3$\,pp band)---the anticipated full-budget coupled dominance did not materialize. One artifact disclosure: the evidence field \texttt{median\_ratio\_D\_over\_C} stores $C/D$ (coupled $\div$ decoupled), i.e.\ the field name is inverted relative to its content; the file is retained unedited post hoc and this note explains the reading. What the replication measures on real data at this scale is the \emph{accuracy} value of decoupling, not its cost value; the $\geq 3\times$ claim stays scoped to the synthetic tier with its matched-solver arms.

\subsection{PSD-NTU-TRANS-002: ST-GCN-scale three-arm replication with a matched-solver control}\label{app:trans002}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}
Protocol ID PSD-NTU-TRANS-002, frozen v1.0 on 2026-09-07, before any run (pre-registration: \texttt{docs/paper/ntu-transition-002-preregistration.md}; driver: \texttt{scripts/run\_r23\_trans002.py}; evidence: \texttt{reports/r23-trans002-*.json}). Motivation: TRANS-001's cost endpoint was arithmetically unpassable at MLP scale (the coupled arm had no retraining cost to save), so this protocol scales the backbone to the full ST-GCN and adds a third arm that separates representation quality from retraining cost.

\paragraph{Data and scenario (frozen).}
Identical to TRANS-001 item by item: the same NTU60 xsub HRNet 2D split (40{,}091 / 16{,}487) and the same frozen ten-merge map $\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}'$ (60 $\to$ 49 classes; Table~\ref{tab:mergemap}), reused unchanged, not redesigned.

\paragraph{Arms (frozen; three arms).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm C$'$ (coupled, ST-GCN end-to-end).} ST-GCN of the same family as the equivalence verification, trained end-to-end supervised from scratch on $\mathcal{Y}'$ labels for 80 epochs. Implementation (registered): the official AimCLR code chain (\texttt{net.st\_gcn.Model} + \texttt{Feeder\_single}, frame-50 export), SGD momentum 0.9, batch 16, lr 0.1, weight decay $10^{-4}$, step schedule [60], official train augmentation.
\item \textbf{Arm D$'$ (decoupled, ST-GCN).} The ST-GCN pretext is trained once, self-supervised (AimCLR objective, 300 epochs, no $\mathcal{Y}'$ labels), then frozen; a StandardScaler + logistic-regression head (tol $10^{-3}$, the E9-series head convention) is retrained on the 256-d penultimate features of the frozen \texttt{epoch300\_model.pt}.
\item \textbf{Arm M (matched-solver control; new).} The \emph{same} solver and head as D$'$ (StandardScaler + logistic regression), but computed on the penultimate features of the \emph{coupled} arm C$'$'s trained checkpoint---``coupled representation, decoupled retraining cost''. D$'$ vs.\ M therefore isolates representation quality at equal (linear-head) retraining cost; M is a mechanism control, not a third method.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Budgets, seeds, accounting (frozen).}
Budget tiers full (40{,}091) and 10\% (4{,}009, seed-42 stratified); seeds 42/43/44 for the cost endpoint. Wall-clock accounting blocks the pretext-amortization dispute: D$'$'s 300-epoch pretext training is reported separately (counted once), and both the head-only caliber (the decision caliber, comparable with E6/TRANS-001) and the amortized caliber are reported; C$'$ is timed per seed per budget with a \texttt{perf\_counter} envelope plus GPU snapshots, and no timing noise is filtered.

\paragraph{Decision rule (frozen).}
\textbf{CONFIRMS}: median wall-clock ratio $C'/D' \geq 3\times$ \emph{and} $|\mathrm{acc}_{D'} - \mathrm{acc}_{C'}| < 2.3$\,pp; \textbf{PARTIAL}: ratio $\geq 3\times$ but accuracy out of band; \textbf{FAILS}: ratio $< 3\times$, in which case the paper's $\geq 3\times$ wording escalates automatically to the stronger negative (``synthetic tier only; ST-GCN-scale replication also fails''). Arm M does not enter the decision rule (protection against post-hoc selection); the registered M reading is: if $\mathrm{acc}_M > \mathrm{acc}_{D'}$ by $\geq 1$\,pp, the end-to-end-trained representation outperforms the frozen self-supervised representation, and part of D$'$'s accuracy disadvantage is attributable to representation rather than paradigm.

\paragraph{Registered event rule and what fired.}
A NaN watchdog was added to the protocol: a run that collapses to a constant predictor is discarded. The first full-budget run at the nominal linearly-scaled learning rate collapsed to a constant predictor mid-training on all three seeds and was discarded under this rule; the reported run uses half the linear-scaled learning rate with the same schedule. All three coupled-arm seeds contain host-restart segments (consumer-host RAM pressure); wall-clock is the sum of contributing segments per seed, with the per-segment decomposition in the released artifact.

\paragraph{Results (post-run, verbatim).}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccccl}
\toprule
Budget & C$'$ (med.) & D$'$ head (med.) & $C'/D'$ & Acc C$'$ & Acc D$'$ & Gap & Verdict \\
\midrule
full & 9{,}897\,s & 626.3\,s & 15.8$\times$ & 79.8\% & 75.3\% & $-4.5$\,pp & PARTIAL \\
10\% & 1{,}520\,s & 47\,s & 32.5$\times$ & 65.0\% & 67.6\% & $+2.6$\,pp & PARTIAL \\
\bottomrule

At full budget the cost ratio clears the $3\times$ line (15.8$\times$; a final-segment-only sensitivity bound gives 6.2$\times$, still $\geq 3\times$) but the accuracy gap ($-4.5$\,pp) falls outside the band, so the frozen rule returns \textbf{PARTIAL}---the ratio and its accuracy price are reported together, as registered. At the 10\% tier the same replication reads 32.5$\times$ (ratio computed on unrounded seconds) with a $+2.6$\,pp decoupled advantage: \textbf{PARTIAL} at the 10\% tier.

\paragraph{Post-hoc robustness expansion (PSD-NTU-TRANS-002-MEXP, dated 2026-09-09, frozen before any new run).}
The addendum extends arms C$'$ and M to ten seeds (42--51; NaN replacement pool 52--55) and upgrades the three-seed M-vs-C$'$ observation to a paired test: per-seed pairs $(M_s - C'_s)$, two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank at $\alpha = 0.05$ plus a sign test, governed as a two-test addendum family with Holm correction inside it. Outcome: M exceeds C$'$ on every seed (mean 82.6\% vs.\ 79.7\%; $+2.9$\,pp; 10/10 sign wins; Wilcoxon $p = 0.002$, Holm-adjusted $p = 0.004$); at the frozen three-seed budget M already led (82.6\% vs.\ 79.8\%). The registered reading: end-to-end training under-exploits its own representation---a linear head retrained on the coupled arm's frozen penultimate features is more accurate than the coupled arm's end-to-end output. Wall-clock does not enter the addendum; the frozen cost verdict (PARTIAL, 15.8$\times$/$-4.5$\,pp at full budget) is unchanged.

\subsection{Synthetic-tier frozen-backbone three-arm contrast}\label{app:threearm}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}
Registered control of the main text's attribution family (Section~4.4, first bullet; the frozen-backbone rows of the attribution-controls table there). Driver: \texttt{scripts/p1\_sprint/frozen\_backbone\_contrast.py}; evidence: \texttt{runs/p1\_sprint/frozen\_backbone\_contrast/summary.json} (mode \texttt{full}, seed 42). Question addressed (reviewer remark on the original submission): is the framework's downstream benefit merely a property of freezing a pretrained backbone---or of the pretrained representation itself---rather than of the semantic-layer mechanism?

\paragraph{Arms (only the encoder's initialization and trainability differ).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm A (frozen).} The AimCLR encoder$_q$ weights are strict-loaded from the released pretext checkpoint recorded in the artifact (\texttt{runs/ntu\_phaseB/joint\_pretext/epoch120\_model.pt}); every encoder parameter is set \texttt{requires\_grad=False}, and only a new linear classification head is trained on top (the repository's adaptation of the official linear-evaluation convention). Freezing is verified mechanically: 6{,}423 trainable parameters (of 926{,}491 total) before \emph{and} after training.
\item \textbf{Arm B (full fine-tuning, ``fullft'').} The same encoder architecture initialized from the same checkpoint, plus the same new head; all 926{,}491 parameters are unfrozen and trained.
\item \textbf{Arm C (scratch).} The same architecture and head with random initialization (constructed under \texttt{torch.manual\_seed(42)}); full fine-tuning. This is the $-$SSL arm of the pretraining ablation family.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Shared data, budget, and stopping rules.}
All three arms consume identical draws from the synthetic 22-class generator (\texttt{psd/data/synth\_stgcn}, the single source of truth for this tier): 50 training samples per class (1{,}100 clips) and 10 validation samples per class (220 clips) from a disjoint generator seed (seed $42+1000$). Training uses one shared configuration: 100 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate $10^{-3}$, validation top-1 evaluated every epoch, early stopping disabled---a fixed 100-epoch budget for every arm, with the reported metric being the best validation top-1 over the run (best-epoch checkpoint selection) and the final-epoch accuracy recomputed post hoc for transparency. The three arms run on the same device (single GPU) under the same trainer and seed (42); no hyperparameter differs across arms by construction.

\paragraph{Results (verbatim from the summary artifact).}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Arm & Best val top-1 & Best epoch & Final-epoch top-1 & Wall-clock & Trainable/total params \\
\midrule
A: frozen & 68.64\% & 35 & 66.82\% & 176.9\,s & 6{,}423 / 926{,}491 \\
B: fullft & 98.18\% & 18 & 97.27\% & 342.3\,s & 926{,}491 / 926{,}491 \\
C: scratch & 97.73\% & 81 & 96.82\% & 365.7\,s & 926{,}491 / 926{,}491 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Reading (the registered interpretation).}
Naive freezing collapses accuracy: the frozen arm reaches 68.64\% against 98.18\% for full fine-tuning ($-29.5$\,pp) while saving only part of the wall-clock (176.9\,s vs.\ 342.3\,s, a $1.9\times$ difference). A generic frozen-backbone-plus-standard-head design therefore does \emph{not} reproduce the decoupled result on this tier, and the $\geq 3\times$ retraining saving is not ``freezing for free'': freezing alone buys a modest speedup at a large accuracy price. The mechanism that preserves accuracy at reduced cost is the semantic-layer rebuild---in E6 (main text, Section~4.3) the decoupled semantic-layer rebuild averages 31.1\,s against 188.7\,s for full-pipeline retraining ($6.07\times$; same-seed paired ratios never below $3.99\times$) with the accuracy difference inside the pre-registered $\pm 2.3$\,pp noise band. Budget caliber: this contrast runs at 50 clips per class (1{,}100 clips) whereas the E6 comparison runs at $n{=}30$/$n{=}100$ clips per class, so the 31.1\,s versus 188.7\,s figures are not same-budget with the arms above; the cross-experiment comparison is mechanistic, not a shared-budget cost ratio (per-seed E6 ratios in Section~\ref{app:e6seeds}). Both full-fine-tuning arms converge to the same ceiling (98.18\% fullft vs.\ 97.73\% scratch), consistent with the gradient-ablation crossover finding of the main text (Section~5): at a saturated budget the pretrained initialization adds no fine-tuning value. The load-bearing comparison for the decoupling claim is thus frozen-plus-standard-head (collapses) versus the semantic rebuild (matches full retraining at lower cost), not freezing alone.

\subsection{Synthetic-tier transition cost (E6): per-seed paired ratios}\label{app:e6seeds}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}

\subsection{Merge/rename zero-retraining upper bound (NTU60)}\label{app:mergebound}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}
Registered control of the main text's attribution family (Section~4.4, second bullet); it appears as the merge/rename-bound rows of the attribution-controls table there. Driver evidence: \texttt{runs/p1\_sprint/merge\_rename\_upper/summary.json} (mode \texttt{full}, seed 42).

\paragraph{Protocol.}
For transitions that only merge or rename classes, no retraining is needed at all, so a zero-retraining baseline is an upper bound on any claimed transition-cost saving for this subclass. The registered episode is a pure merge of the NTU60 label space into ten coarse super-classes (60 $\to$ 10; the ten-class coarse-grouping episode reported in the main text's attribution table): xsub split (40{,}091 train / 16{,}487 val), 10\% labeled budget (4{,}009 clips, seed-42 stratified). Two frozen transforms are applied to the frozen $\mathcal{Y}$-era model: the \emph{rename-oracle upper bound} sums source-class posteriors through the static merge map (zero retraining; assumes error-free map lookup), and the \emph{argmax-vote lower bound} hard-votes the source argmax predictions (the difference from score summation is exactly the calibration loss). Features are the frozen epoch-300 joint pretext's 256-d penultimate; the classifier is the E9-series ScaledLR (StandardScaler + logistic regression, \texttt{max\_iter}=1000) reused unchanged; the reference is a fully supervised linear head trained on the same labeled subset under the target ten-class taxonomy. This episode is distinct from the $60\to49$ ten-merge episode of the TRANS-001/002 replications (Sections~\ref{app:trans001}--\ref{app:trans002}); the bound and the transition replications therefore appear side by side in the main text's attribution table as registered controls on different calibers, not as a same-episode comparison.

\paragraph{Results (verbatim).}
Rename-oracle upper bound 76.76\% top-1 (76.8\% in the main text); argmax-vote lower bound 76.51\% (76.5\%); fully supervised ten-class reference 71.5\% (retention of the bound over the reference: 107.4\%). For pure merge/rename transitions the zero-retraining remap of the frozen model exceeds even full retraining of the head on the merged taxonomy, confirming that the bound is binding: any rebuild-based approach (including the decoupled semantic rebuild) must be read against this zero-cost baseline rather than only against full retraining, exactly as the main text's attribution discussion states.

\section{Downstream ablation of the SMQ segmentation step}\label{app:smq}

\paragraph{Status and provenance.}
The main text downgrades motion-word quantization (SMQ)~\citep{gokay2025smq} to an auxiliary proposal front-end (Section~3.2.2) and registers a controlled downstream ablation to quantify its contribution rather than asserting it by framing. Protocol driver: \texttt{scripts/p1\_sprint/smq\_downstream\_ablation.py}; evidence: \texttt{runs/p1\_sprint/smq\_downstream\_ablation/summary.json} (mode \texttt{full}, seed 42).

\paragraph{Design (same data, same labels, same classifier; only the segmentation step differs).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Arm \emph{with} SMQ.} Each clip is run through SMQ inference (the released P0.2 epoch-30 checkpoint, the end-to-end $K{=}8$ configuration of the segmentation qualitative figure) to obtain per-frame motion-word indices; the raw skeleton frames are grouped into contiguous same-code segments, and each segment's frames are averaged into one sub-sequence unit (resampled to the extractor's 30-frame caliber). The clip representation pools the unit features (frame-level $\to$ segment-level compression).
\item \textbf{Arm \emph{without} SMQ.} The same skeleton sequence is split directly into equal-length units, with the per-clip unit count aligned to the SMQ arm's segment count (equal representation capacity), pooled identically.
\end{itemize}
Both arms share the same frozen ST-GCN+BC feature extractor (the p07 warm checkpoint, penultimate features) and the same unit-level logistic-regression classifier (\texttt{max\_iter}=2000, $C{=}1.0$); the clip-level score is the mean of unit posteriors, deliberately decoupled from the segment count so the two arms are comparable.

\paragraph{Supervision caliber (honest disclosure).}
InterPet4D carries no behavior ground truth. Clip labels are rule-seed pseudo-ground-truth dominant classes: the frame-label majority over frames with rule confidence $\geq 0.8$ (the released rule-seed files, \texttt{data/seeds/rule\_seeds}). The ablation therefore measures whether SMQ segmentation improves \emph{clip-level behavioral separability under pseudo-labels} on the public-real tier; its numbers are never pooled with ground-truth benchmarks.

\paragraph{Data and split.}
All 225 labeled InterPet4D clips; the pseudo-label vocabulary covers seven classes (jump, lying, rise\_transition, running, sitting, standing, walking); a deterministic stratified split assigns the last third of each class to validation: 151 train / 74 val, under seed 42.

\paragraph{Result (verbatim).}
Validation top-1: \textbf{45.95\% with SMQ vs.\ 45.95\% without SMQ}---$\Delta = 0.0$ exactly, on identical clips, labels, features, and classifier (single full run, seed 42; 48.1\,s wall-clock).

\paragraph{Reading.}
Under this registered protocol, replacing SMQ's same-code segments with capacity-matched uniform windows leaves downstream clip-level accuracy unchanged ($\Delta = 0.0$). This closes the loop with the boundary-metric picture of the main text (Section~5): SMQ leads on boundary IoU (0.458 vs.\ 0.399) but trails on boundary F1 (0.343 vs.\ 0.396), and a fixed-grid probe is statistically on par (0.453)---so neither the boundary metrics nor this downstream ablation shows a consistent SMQ advantage. The result supports the paper's positioning of SMQ as an auxiliary, non-load-bearing proposal source: the claims of the main text do not rest on it. Scope: this is a controlled ablation of the \emph{segmentation step} under frozen features and a linear head, at one SMQ operating point (epoch-30 checkpoint, $K{=}8$) on the public-real tier; it is not by itself the full end-to-end pipeline attribution (rerunning the entire pseudo-label loop with and without SMQ), which remains registered future work. Within this caliber, the segmentation choice is a measured operating decision, not a load-bearing component.

\section{Relation to class-incremental learning}\label{app:cil}

The main text positions this work against class-incremental learning (CIL) and continual learning in its related-work section; this note restates the positioning for readers of the supplement alone. Taxonomy evolution under a frozen backbone is adjacent to CIL~\citep{rebuffi2017icarl,wang2024continual,masana2023cil}: both settings reuse frozen or slowly updated representations, retrain heads, and reconcile logits across label spaces (classifier realignment; logit aggregation for merged heads). The objectives differ, however. CIL methods are designed and benchmarked for incremental accuracy over sequences of arriving, disjoint class sets, typically spending rehearsal buffers, distillation, or parameter isolation to resist catastrophic forgetting; this paper asks for the \emph{cost--benefit accounting} of an explicitly declared taxonomy remap $\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Y}'$ over an already-collected unlabeled stream, where the semantic layer rebuilds coverage from rule-engine seeds plus unsupervised proposals and no rehearsal or memory-preservation objective is in play. CIL baselines therefore answer a question the registered transition protocol does not ask---accuracy retention across incremental sessions under an arriving-class stream---and are not comparable within the matched-budget, frozen-remap design without re-experimentation; conversely, the transition-cost endpoints reported here (wall-clock and annotation units at matched accuracy under a frozen remap) are not quantities CIL benchmarks report. The two lines are complementary: CIL supplies mechanisms for absorbing \emph{arriving} classes under stability--plasticity trade-offs, while this paper measures when the decoupled structure makes an \emph{arbitrary declared remap} worth absorbing at low cost. Scope: the measured episodes cover merges and renames (the TRANS-001/002 replications and the E1 bound) and, through the E3 episode above, splits and additions on the synthetic tier (the new-class accuracy ladder runs from 0\% for the zero-shot remap to 100\% under coupled full retraining).
