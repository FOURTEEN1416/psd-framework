ABSTRACT
Evaluation criteria in behavior recognition keep evolving; taxonomy changes re-open annotation and retraining. We propose PSD, a physics-semantics decoupled framework for skeletonbased behavior recognition: a label-free physics layer learns how skeletons move; a semantic layer learns what behaviors are called from rule-engine seeds via clustering, confidencefiltered pseudo-labels, and warm-started self-training. A pre-registered, tier-separated protocol reports cost against accuracy and publishes failures. A synthetic-tier taxonomy transition is absorbed at 3× lower retraining cost than full retraining (6.07×). Controls attribute the savings to semantic rebuilding, not freezing (naive freezing: 68.6% vs. 98.2%). On the real merge transition the decoupled rebuild (75.3%) trails equal-budget alternatives (coupled 79.8%, matched-solver 82.6%); the same-episode zero-retraining bound reads 68.0% (10% head budget). On split/addition transitions, a labeled-budget ladder (10-100 labels/class) lifts the decoupled rebuild from 45.3% to 72.4% versus coupled 95.9%--outside the ±2.3 pp parity band: the residual-value hypothesis is not supported at any tested budget. The warm-started semantic layer reaches 82.0% top-1 on 22 classes from 20 clips (vs. 96.6% full-budget). A frozen-feature pipeline retains 90.7% of full-budget linear-probe accuracy at 10% labels on NTU60 (75.0% NTU120 pre-registered physics layer; 88.9% interim supervised encoder; 82.5% PanAf500, label-free pretext)--above re-implemented AimCLR (67.5% vs. 66.7%) and supervised ST-GCN (64.8%), though full-budget supervision dominates (83.5%). It collapses on UCF101 (51.1%) and stays below chance on the canine tier at 13% budget (9.8% vs. 11.1%). Decoupling pays where a frozen backbone is reusable and semantics rebuild cheaply; the protocol exposes where it fails.

1. Introduction
Deployed behavior-recognition pipelines rarely face a fixed definition of what they recognize. Working-dog programs redefine behavior categories as training standards evolve; welfare protocols split or merge classes as regulations change; veterinary research adopts finer ethograms as instruments improve. Each such revision re-opens the annotation and retraining pipeline behind the classifier. The economics are unforgiving: training a working dog costs tens of thousands of dollars per animal, and more than half of candidates fail to graduate (Grimm, 2026), so the measurement layer around these programs is both expensive and subject to recurring re-specification.
This paper studies a structural response to that recurring cost: factorize the pipeline so that the part that must track the taxonomy is small. We examine the physics-semantics decoupling pattern, PSD, for skeleton-based behavior recognition under evolving evaluation criteria. A physics layer  learns how skeletons move without behavior labels: self-supervised pretraining adapted to quadruped topology plus unsupervised motion-word segmentation that cuts streams into behavior proposals. A semantic layer  learns what behaviors are called from a small seed budget: ruleengine coarse labels seed class anchors, prototype clustering assigns pseudo-labels under confidence filtering, and

The question this paper answers is deliberately narrower than "does decoupling work?". Any design that freezes a backbone and retrains a head inherits the same computational shortcut at a taxonomy change, so the wall-clock ratio alone does not justify the mechanism; and a pre-registered real-domain replication shows the shortcut can invert at small scale, where there is no retraining cost left to save (Section 4, E6-real). We therefore treat decoupling as a cost-benefit hypothesis with explicit boundary conditions: it pays where a frozen physics layer remains reusable and semantics are cheap to rebuild, and it fails where pseudo-label quality collapses. The contribution is a measured boundary (where the cost advantage holds, where accuracy holds, and where the pipeline breaks), established under a pre-registered, tier-separated protocol whose decision rules, tolerances, and negative results are reported in full.
Why animal skeletons, and where the boundary lies. Animal behavior recognition motivates the problem: labeled behavior data is scarce, skeletons are the practical signal, and evaluation criteria in working-dog and welfare settings evolve with operational practice (Section 2.1). On the only public canine behavior tier we could assemble, the endto-end pipeline stays below chance at a 13% annotation budget (9.8% ± 7.5 against an 11.1% chance level, with a generic-SSL control reaching 15.2% ± 5.1), and a pre-registered diagnosis attributes the failure to label misalignment in the sole public source of canine behavior video with action labels, not to skeleton quality (Section 4, E7; Section 6, L9). We report this as a boundary condition, have pre-registered the target-domain working-dog pilot for a later study, and scope every positive claim to the tiers where it was actually measured rather than claiming a general solution to animal behavior recognition.
Our contributions are threefold:
1. A cost-benefit boundary for physics-semantics decoupling under taxonomy evolution. On the synthetic tier, a taxonomy transition is absorbed at  3× lower wall-clock retraining cost than full-pipeline retraining at matched accuracy (conservative bound backed by a measured 6.07×; same-seed paired ratios never below 3.99×). This bound is a synthetic-tier measurement: real-domain replications return FAIL at MLP scale (no cost to save) and PARTIAL at backbone scale (cost line cleared, accuracy band not exactly matched), so the claim is scoped, not universal. A pre-registered merge/rename analysis sharpens the boundary. For transitions expressible as label remaps or logit aggregation, zero-retraining upper bounds apply. On the one real merge transition we measure, a matched-solver control bounds the decoupled rebuild from above (82.6% vs. 75.3%), and the zeroretraining remap bound measured on the same episode reads 68.0%, below the rebuild; the rebuild is therefore not dominated by the zero-cost path on this episode. A synthetic split/addition episode probes the residual value of decoupling where new supervision is unavoidable: the zero-retraining remap cannot name the new classes (0%), and the decoupled rebuild recovers 45.7% overall with 10 new labels per class, at 3.0× lower median wall-clock and 5× fewer new labels than full retraining (97.1%; a frozen head with the full new-label budget: 68.0%). A labeled-budget ladder on the same transition type (  {10, 25, 50, 100} fresh labels per new class) raises the decoupled arm from 45.3% to 72.4% (three seeds 42-44 per rung) but saturates at the frozen-feature ceiling (frozen-head arm: 71.7%) and never approaches the coupled arm (95.9%). The smallest margin, 23.45 pp at =100, sits far outside the ±2.3 pp parity band, so the residual-value hypothesis fails across the entire tested ladder; decoupling's measured value in this regime is label and wall-clock savings, and closing the gap appears to require adapting the frozen encoder (seeds 42-44; Section 4). The cost of freezing itself is likewise measured. A frozen-encoder control on the three real E9-series tiers (NTU120, UCF101, PanAf500) shows naive freezing retains only 88.9%/66.6%/85.8% of full-budget accuracy under the interim supervised encoder, and a protocolreturn amendment executing the pre-registered label-free physics layer on NTU120, UCF101, and PanAf500 reads 75.0%/51.1%/82.5% (all FAILS; Section 4.4).
2. A transparent, pre-registered evaluation protocol for tier-separated claims. Three tiers (synthetic, publicreal, real-K9) plus public benchmarks are never pooled; every comparison carries its caliber (label space, budget definition, device, seed dispersion); cost is reported as a pair (annotation units, wall-clock) at matched accuracy; and decision rules (including the conservative  3× bound, the FULL/PARTIAL/FAIL verdicts, and Holmcorrected families) are frozen before runs. Negative results are first-class findings: the canine-tier collapse below chance, the null rescue levers, and the retention collapse on weak-reference domains (UCF101 66.6% interim encoder; 51.1% label-free) are reported under the same rules that certify the positive results.

2. Related work
Animal behavior recognition supports applications from livestock welfare monitoring to working-dog training. Training a single service dog costs tens of thousands of dollars, and more than half of candidates fail to graduate (Grimm, 2026), motivating objective, automated behavior assessment. Deployed systems already combine wearable sensors with classical machine learning for canine posture estimation (Marcato et al., 2023), yet the field lacks a recognition framework that works when labels are scarce and evaluation criteria keep evolving. We review three bodies of work bearing on this gap--animal behavior understanding, skeleton-based self-supervised learning, and

2.1. Animal behavior understanding Existing animal behavior pipelines divide into appearance-based, pose-based, and sensor-based lines. Appearance-based methods classify behavior directly from RGB frames or video. YOLO-PetX adapts a detection
backbone to anomalous dog behavior (Xu et al., 2025). TP-CanineNet applies temporal contrastive learning with pseudo-labels to canine separation-anxiety video (Guo et al., 2026). These methods inherit the strengths of image recognition but depend on large annotated corpora, and their accuracy degrades under background change, occlusion by handlers, and breed variation in appearance.
Pose-based methods first extract keypoints, then classify dynamics. DeepLabCut (Mathis et al., 2018) and SLEAP (Pereira et al., 2022) made markerless animal pose estimation practical. Building on such inputs, ASBAR unifies skeleton-based animal action recognition with PoseConv3D and reports 75.3% top-1 on primate benchmarks (Fuchs et al., 2024); BCST-GCN adapts ST-GCN-style graph convolutions with bidirectional cross-attention to pig behavior, reaching 95.36% on its four-behavior dataset (Chai et al., 2026); hierarchical representation learning of dog behavior from single-view 3D pose has also been explored (Miyai et al., 2025). Skeleton representations are compact, background-invariant, and robust across individuals--properties that should suit low-resource settings--but every classifier above is trained fully supervised on a fixed label set.
Sensor-based methods use accelerometers or IMUs. They capture coarse daily activities reliably (Marcato et al., 2023) but cannot resolve fine-grained behaviors that differ mainly in body configuration rather than global motion.
Public data landscape. The scarcity that motivates this work is visible in the data that actually exists. Among the datasets we could identify, the only large-scale real animal-behavior video benchmark with per-video action labels is Animal Kingdom (Ng et al., 2022), in which dogs appear as a sparsely-labeled subset; canine-specific resources are otherwise wearable inertial time series with ethogram-annotated behaviors (Vehkaoja et al., 2022; Marcato et al., 2023), synthetic dog videos built for pose estimation (Shooter et al., 2024), or pose-only still images. To our knowledge none provides real working-dog behavior as skeleton sequences with per-segment expert action labels at benchmark scale. By "scarce" we mean skeleton-registered expert behavior labels: Animal Kingdom provides frame-level action labels but no 3D skeletons, so obtaining skeleton sequences requires self-extraction--it is the joint availability of clean skeletons and per-segment action labels that is missing. Our public-real tier is accordingly assembled by self-extraction from Animal Kingdom (Section 4.1), and the target-domain real-K9 tier is pre-registered yet left unexercised. On our own evidence (Section 5; the alignment analysis in Section 6, L9) the negative end-to-end boundary on the canine tier is attributable to label misalignment and extraction quality in the only public source of canine behavior video with action labels; whether it also reflects true domain-level scarcity is precisely the question the pre-registered K9 pilot is designed to separate.
Gap. Current animal pipelines assume a fixed taxonomy and supervision-heavy training. Animal pose selfsupervision is no longer unexplored: TREBA learns rodent behavior representations from a fraction of expert labels by programming pretext tasks from domain knowledge (Sun et al., 2021), and h/BehaveMAE extends masked autoencoders to spatio-temporal motion-capture sequences (Stoffl et al., 2024); domain-adaptive pretraining improves primate behavior recognition (Mueller et al., 2025), and self-supervised video pretraining extends to multi-animal settings (Sun et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025b). What these lines share is that they stop at representations and their evaluation probes: none couples them to temporal behavior proposals, to a label-expansion loop, or to a mechanism for absorbing taxonomy change.
2.2. Skeleton-based self-supervised and unsupervised learning Self-supervised representation learning for skeletons is mature in the human domain. ST-GCN established graph
convolutions over joint topologies (Yan et al., 2018), and PoseConv3D recasts keypoints as heatmap volumes for 3D convolution (Duan et al., 2022). Among unsupervised objectives, AimCLR maximizes asymmetry across extreme augmented views with an InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2018) queue (Guo et al., 2022): under the linear-evaluation protocol (frozen encoder + FC-softmax classifier; three-stream joint/motion/bone fusion), the conference paper reports 78.9% on NTU60 cross-subject, the official repository's re-measurement gives 79.18%, and its journal extension is credited with 80.9% in the official results table (Guo et al., 2024). Cross-view contrastive objectives form a parallel family (Li et al., 2021); we treat the encoder choice as replaceable within our framework, with AimCLR as the reference physics-layer instantiation rather than a load-bearing commitment. For temporal structure, unsupervised segmentation methods quantize motion into discrete vocabularies; a motion-word quantization pipeline (SMQ) derives behaviorproposal boundaries from quantized motion-word sequences (Gökay et al., 2025).

Two observations motivate our treatment. First, virtually all benchmarks, augmentations, and graph templates assume the human body plan; transferring these objectives to quadruped kinematics requires adapting joint topology, augmentation priors, and view construction. Second, pretraining and segmentation are studied separately, although a recognition system needs both: representations without proposals cannot localize behaviors, and proposals without semantics cannot name them. State-space backbones such as VideoMamba (Li et al., 2024) and Mamba-MSQNet (Fazzari et al., 2024) offer efficiency alternatives but do not address label scarcity either.
Gap. Self-supervised skeleton machinery is mature for humans and, since TREBA and h/BehaveMAE (Sun et al., 2021; Stoffl et al., 2024), has entered the animal domain--but as representation learning, validated through probes and clustering, still decoupled from unsupervised temporal decomposition and from any label-expansion loop; our repository-scale survey found no published animal-skeleton pipeline combining self-supervised representation learning with the latter two. In the human domain, masked autoencoders (Wu et al., 2022) and foundation-model efforts (Wang et al., 2025a) pretrain representations for broad transfer; our decoupling claim is orthogonal--the frozen component is the physics encoder, the revisable component is the taxonomy-facing semantic layer, and the measured quantity is taxonomy-transition cost rather than representation quality (full survey matrix in the supplementary material).
2.3. Semi-supervised, few-shot, and pseudo-label learning Semi-supervised skeleton action recognition in the human domain relies mostly on consistency regularization
and teacher-student schemes, including momentum-contrastive teachers (Lu et al., 2025) and graph-representation alignment (Huang et al., 2024); these regularize predictions under perturbation but receive no external guidance about which classes matter. MAC-Learning introduces anchor-based contrastive learning, where "anchors" are contrastive sample pairs (Shu et al., 2023); it maintains no seed-annotated anchors supervising cluster assignment and includes no iterative pseudo-label loop. Closest in mechanism, ProSL iterates between clustering and pseudo-label generation in self-supervised contrastive learning (Qin et al., 2025): it targets fully unsupervised representation learning on human benchmarks, whereas PSD runs a semi-supervised recognition loop driven by externally supplied rule-engine seed anchors--the semantic layer, not the representation, is the object being trained--and adds confidence filtering, temporal behavior proposals, and the taxonomy-transition cost framing. Point-supervised skeleton action segmentation is another close neighbor, generating prototype-similarity pseudo-labels with clustering from one labeled frame per segment (Wang et al., 2026); our combination differs in rule-engine seed anchors, confidence-filtered iteration, and the transition-cost framing--we claim firstness for that combination, not for prototype pseudo-labels per se. Few-shot approaches such as PAINet learn episode-based metric classifiers (Liu et al., 2023) and likewise lack a mechanism for expanding labels beyond the support set.
Vision foundation models supply another route to low-shot recognition (Caron et al., 2021). Skeleton-to-image encoding maps skeletons into images consumed by VFM-pretrained encoders (Yang et al., 2026), demonstrating that foundation-model features transfer to skeleton data. This line uses VFMs for representation only; it does not close the loop from a handful of annotated seeds to full taxonomy coverage. In the animal domain specifically, TP-CanineNet is the closest use of pseudo-labels, operating on RGB video rather than skeletal dynamics (Guo et al., 2026).

TREBA (Sun et al., 2021) (CVPR 2021). What it does. Task programming injects domain knowledge into selfsupervised pretext tasks (frame-behavior affinity, temporal-order prediction) so that rodent behavior representations can be learned from limited expert annotations; task programming approaches expert-level labeling efficiency on flycourtship and mouse home-cage videos. Where we differ. TREBA learns an embedding for RGB/pose frames under a fixed ethogram; it has no temporal behavior-proposal stage, no mechanism that converts embeddings into taxonomywide labels, and no notion of an evaluation taxonomy that later changes. PSD increment. PSD uses its label-free physics layer as a substrate: the semantic layer turns embeddings plus unsupervised proposals into labels under the current taxonomy, and the measured quantity is the cost of changing that taxonomy, not probe accuracy.
h/BehaveMAE (Stoffl et al., 2024) (ECCV 2024). What it does. A hierarchical masked autoencoder for animal motion-capture data (mice, macaques, ASTM) learns spatio-temporal behavior representations without labels; probes show lower layers encode fine-grained movements, higher layers complex activities. Where we differ. h/BehaveMAE is a representation-quality study: its outputs are latents and probe accuracies, not a recognition pipeline--it does not segment streams into proposals, expand labels, or consider taxonomy evolution. PSD increment. PSD treats such encoders as the replaceable frozen physics layer (Section 2.2) and contributes the missing half: a revisable semantic layer and an explicit account of what happens--to accuracy and wall-clock cost--when the taxonomy changes.
ProSL (Qin et al., 2025) (Machine Learning 2025). What it does. ProSL improves unsupervised skeleton-based action recognition by iterating between clustering and pseudo-label generation: a contrastively pretrained encoder is clustered into a codebook, cluster assignments serve as pseudo-labels, and the loop progressively refines the representation; it reports the strongest published unsupervised results on human skeleton benchmarks. Where we differ. ProSL's pseudo-labels are cluster identities training the encoder; there are no externally supplied seeds, so the label space is the clustering's own--nothing connects it to a domain taxonomy, to behavior proposals, or to a transitioncost question. PSD increment. PSD inverts the direction of trust: representations are frozen after pretraining, and pseudo-labels grow a semantic layer from rule-engine seed anchors toward a prescribed taxonomy under confidence filtering; the evaluated quantities are low-budget recognition accuracy and taxonomy-transition cost. This is also why ProSL cannot be transplanted as a baseline into our three-tier protocol without re-experimentation (Table 3 of the supplementary material).
Relation to class-incremental and continual learning. Taxonomy evolution under a frozen backbone is adjacent to class-incremental learning (CIL) and continual learning (Rebuffi et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2024; Masana et al., 2023): both share machinery--frozen or slowly updated representations, head re-training, and logit-level reconciliation across label spaces--but the objectives differ. CIL is designed and benchmarked for incremental accuracy under sequences of disjoint class sets, typically spending rehearsal buffers, distillation, or parameter isolation to resist forgetting (Rebuffi et al., 2017; Masana et al., 2023); PSD asks for the cost-benefit accounting of an explicitly declared taxonomy remap    over an already-collected unlabeled stream, with no rehearsal or memory-preservation objective in play. CIL baselines therefore answer a question our protocol does not ask and are not comparable within our matched-budget, frozen-remap design without re-experimentation; conversely, our transition-cost endpoints are not quantities CIL benchmarks report. The two lines are complementary: CIL supplies mechanisms for absorbing arriving classes under stability-plasticity trade-offs, while this paper measures when the decoupled structure makes an arbitrary declared remap--merges and renames here; splits and additions on the synthetic tier (Section 4)--worth absorbing at low cost. A fuller restatement for readers of the supplement alone is given in the supplementary material.

Gap. Three absences compound across the three lines reviewed above. Animal behavior understanding offers no label-efficient path and no mechanism for taxonomy change (Section 2.1). Skeleton-based self-supervision supplies representation machinery that has begun to reach animals (TREBA, h/BehaveMAE) but stops at representations, and treats segmentation as a separate problem (Section 2.2). Semi-supervised learning supplies pseudo-label machinery-- most closely ProSL's cluster-iteration--whose components have, to our knowledge, never been assembled for temporalskeleton recognition under externally supplied seed anchors (Section 2.3). No surveyed method couples a label-free physics layer (self-supervised dynamics modeling plus unsupervised segmentation) with an anchor-guided semantic layer that grows from tens of seeds to a complete, revisable taxonomy. This coupling is the design whose cost-benefit boundary this paper measures.

3. Method
This section presents the Physics-Semantics Decoupled framework (PSD). Section 3.1 fixes notation and gives an overview. Section 3.2 details the physics layer, which learns how skeletons move without any behavior labels. Section 3.3 details the semantic layer, which learns what behaviors are called from a small seed budget. Section 3.4 formalizes the decoupling mechanism that absorbs evolving evaluation criteria.

Framework overview. PSD factorizes recognition into two layers with a narrow interface. 3.2. Physics layer 3.2.1. Self-supervised pretraining
We adapt extreme-asymmetry contrastive pretraining (Guo et al., 2022) to quadruped skeletons. Three adaptations matter. First, the joint graph is rebuilt for the target species topology: a 24-joint quadruped layout mapped into an NTUcompatible view via an identity correspondence plus one dead slot. Second, augmentation primitives are restricted to quadruped-plausible transformations. Third, the official weight initialization is deliberately skipped: re-initializing all weights to  (0, 0.02) collapses representations into a cone from which InfoNCE cannot escape on our data, and skipping it restores normal convergence--a reproducibility note for practitioners reusing official code bases.
3.2.2. Unsupervised temporal segmentation Continuous skeleton streams must be cut into behavior-proposal units before labeling. We use motion-word
quantization (Gökay et al., 2025) as an auxiliary front-end, not as a contribution claim: local motion patterns are quantized into a discrete vocabulary, and segment boundaries are proposed where the quantized sequence changes regime.
Interface to the physics encoder. SMQ consumes the same species-adapted skeleton view that the physics layer is trained on--SMAL-native 24-joint coordinates resampled to a fixed window--rather than the NTU-compatible 25slot view used inside AimCLR; a dedicated adapter performs the view conversion, so the two components share data provenance but neither consumes the other's internal representations. SMQ delivers only segment boundaries: every proposal is encoded by the frozen  into a dynamics embedding (the same features the semantic layer consumes), and no SMQ-internal codebook or quantizer state enters . 3.3. Semantic layer
The semantic layer grows labeled coverage toward completeness of the taxonomy from the rule-engine seed set , following the anchor-cluster-pseudo-label recipe transferred from image-domain few-shot recognition (Snell et al., 2017) (Section 2.3).
The transfer required re-engineering, not re-application, because three domain shifts bind: First, anchors must be defined over temporal segment embeddings from a self-supervised physics encoder instead of image features, so anchor quality depends on representation adequacy (Section 3.2.1). Second, prototype clustering operates on variable-length proposals delivered by unsupervised segmentation rather than fixed-size inputs, coupling label-expansion quality to segmentation quality (Section 3.2.2). Third, confidence calibration must tolerate quadruped kinematics whose intraclass motion variance differs from both image textures and human locomotion. Each shift is validated separately in Sections 4-5 before the composed pipeline is evaluated.

3.3.1. Seed anchors and their verification Rule-engine coarse labels over physical priors provide seed anchors  = {(, )}, where  are physics-layer
embeddings of seed segments and  are the engine's coarse labels. Anchors are the only labeled objects the loop consumes; unlike anchor-based contrastive objectives that treat anchors as sample pairs (Shu et al., 2023), our anchors carry semantic identity and directly supervise cluster assignment.

Seeds are priors, not ground truth. The rule engine is deliberately tuned for high recall at the cost of precision: it triggers on physical priors (paw-height ordering for gait classes, head-cluster height for posture classes) and emits a per-segment confidence from rule margin, with short ambiguous spans merged into high-confidence neighbors. We therefore treat every seed label  as a noisy prior, never as ground truth, in three ways. (1) Quality gating at ingestion: seed segments enter  only if the engine's confidence is at least 0.8 and the segment spans at least 0.5 s; unknown segments are discarded. (2) Verification before write-back: only seed labels that survive a human spot-check enter the verified anchor set used by the consolidation step (Section 3.3.3); unverified seeds supervise only the expansion loop, whose confidence gate is calibrated to tolerate their noise. (3) Noise robustness by construction: anchors initialized from noisy seeds are corrected by confidence-filtered cluster consensus and audited in downstream evaluation (Section 5 reports the seed-noise injection study).

What seed verification costs. The verification budget is part of the annotation accounting and is never free: verifying one seed clip means a human auditor watches the segment and confirms or corrects its coarse label--a binary decision per segment, an order of magnitude cheaper than full frame-level annotation of the same clip. Why the same pipeline holds elsewhere: a domain-shift reading. The canine failure is specific to the data regime, not to the architecture: the identical corrected protocol retains 90.7% (NTU60), 88.9% (NTU120, interim supervised encoder; 74.96% under the pre-registered label-free pretext), 85.8% (PanAf500, thirty seeds; 82.5% under the preregistered label-free pretext), and 66.6% (UCF101, interim supervised encoder; 51.09% under the pre-registered labelfree pretext) of full-budget accuracy at 10% labels (E9 series), and the supervised canine references are modest but above chance (33.93% full supervision; 41.5% four-class). We read the gap through three coupled shifts. (i) Labelgranularity shift: canine labels are one behavior per multi-behavior clip, so frame-accurate skeletons pair with clip-level labels whose boundaries disagree with the physics layer's proposals; pool pseudo-label precision averages 0.11 (E7), so self-training amplifies noise. PanAf500, the animal tier that works, votes frame labels to clips only after its clips are behavior-homogeneous, and its full-budget reference (60.0%) is linearly separable. (ii) Pretext-target domain shift: the physics layer is pretrained on mocap-derived InterPet4D skeletons and must transfer to in-the-wild canine video; the generic-SSL control, trained on mocap dog skeletons closer to its own evaluation preprocessing, overtakes the taskpretrained initialization at low budget (15.2% vs. 9.8%); the warm-start advantage was an artifact of the withdrawn true-label-head protocol, and at this budget neither initialization produces usable pseudo-labels. (iii) Class-structure shift: the canine class space is long-tailed with confusable rare classes (jump/stay at 0%), so confidence-filtered selection collapses onto majority classes; the same filter is benign on the balanced human taxonomies. Consistent with this reading, the E9 pretext-learnability gradient predicts collapse precisely where the full-budget reference is weak (UCF101, 23.1%); the canine failure is the same mechanism at a more extreme data-quality regime. The pre-registered working-dog pilot (PSD-K9-PREREG-001) will test whether target-domain data of adequate quality removes the boundary; until then, the low-resource and decoupling claims are scoped to the tiers measured here, and the canine tier is reported as a falsifying boundary condition, not as supporting evidence.

On the synthetic-offset tier (pre-registered offset distribution, noise_std = 0.10), initializing the semantic layer from full-budget weights makes a 20-clip budget usable: 82.0% ± 4.3 top-1 on 22 classes (three seeds), against 95.7% for the same protocol at the 200-clip budget point (91% of the 220-clip offset pool), i.e. 86% of full-budget accuracy from 9% of the labels, and against 41.2% for the frozen, un-adapted backbone on the same offset validation set, so warm-start fine-tuning nearly doubles the un-adapted model (1.99×) under distribution shift. E6: taxonomy-transition cost (C1). This experiment tests whether absorbing a taxonomy change under decoupling costs less than full-pipeline retraining at matched accuracy. On the synthetic tier, under a pre-registered synthetic    remap (released with the code), the baseline arm retrains the full ST-GCN pipeline while the decoupled arm freezes the -era backbone and rebuilds only the semantic layer, three seeds per budget (small: =30 clips, CPU; full: =100 clips, GPU), identical annotation units across arms. The decoupled semantic-layer rebuild averages 31.1 s of wall-clock retraining versus 188.7 s for full-pipeline retraining (6.07×; same-seed paired ratios never below 3.99×; =3, seeds 42-44, per-seed ratios in the supplementary material); convergence epochs improve by a further 2.18×; and accuracy differences fall inside the pre-registered noise band (-0.91 pp full / +2.27 pp small, both < 2.3 pp). Annotation units are identical across arms by construction, so the saving is computational. Following our pre-registered conservative-bound rule we claim  3× lower retraining cost on the synthetic tier, with accuracy equivalence stated as statistical equivalence. The absolute wall-clock values are benchmark-specific; the claim concerns the ratio. Two attribution boundaries--that the ratio is available to any design that freezes a backbone and retrains its head, and that for merge/rename transitions the zero-retraining upper bound (label remap or logit summation) can dominate any rebuild-based approach--are quantified by the pre-registered controls of Section 4.4 (Table 2). The decoupled design's contribution at a transition is therefore the frozen path's accuracy and label economics, compared as a cost-accuracy pair: the semantic layer rebuilds from rule-engine seeds plus unsupervised proposals rather than full re-annotation of  (the E6 protocol maps the same labeled units to both arms, so the seed-budget advantage of  is exercised by the small-budget arms instead).
E6-real: taxonomy-transition cost on the human benchmark (pre-registered replication). A pre-registered replication (PSD-NTU-TRANS-001, frozen before any run) transfers this two-arm design to NTU60 under a frozen ten-merge transition (60  49 classes), with the identical joint-level MLP backbone in both arms (E9b/c convention; decoupled = frozen Y-era pretext plus a linear head retrained on  labels; coupled = the same architecture retrained end-to-end from scratch; seeds 42-44). Because this transition is a pure merge, the zero-retraining upper bound of Section 4.4 applies in principle; the registered control measuring this bound on a ten-class coarse-grouping episode (60  10) is reported in Table 2. The pre-registered cost endpoint fails: the decoupled arm's head retraining (the E9-series CPU logistic-regression solver, 466 s median) is not cheaper than end-to-end retraining of the small coupled MLP (35 s), a 13× cost asymmetry at full budget (2.3× at 10% labels) whose substantive reading is scale: when the entire coupled pipeline retrains in 35 s, there is little retraining cost for decoupling to save; the frozen protocol pinned architecture, epochs, budgets, and seeds but not solver families, and we do not re-run with a matched solver post hoc. The pre-registered accuracy expectation is not confirmed as stated: the protocol anticipated coupled dominance at full supervision and decoupled dominance at 10% labels; the decoupled arm was in fact more accurate at every budget (+3.9 pp at full, +11.3 pp at 10% labels, both outside the ±2.3 pp band); the anticipated coupled dominance did not materialize, with the solver-family confound applying to both endpoints. On real data the measured value of the decoupled pattern at this scale is accuracy, not wall-clock, and per the frozen decision rule the  3× claim remains scoped to the synthetic tier with its matched-solver arms.

The gradient claim is accordingly conditioned: within a fixed physics layer, retention is monotone in full-budget linear-probe reference quality, with the high band (85-90%) holding where the reference is separable and collapse where it is not. The low-resource claim is therefore scoped to tiers whose pretext features support linear separability at full budget, and the retention line is not claimed as a domain-universal property.
E9d: first animal-domain public benchmark (PanAf500, pre-registered). The mechanism boundary above predicts that retention depends on what the pretext learned, not on taxonomy, so it should extend beyond the human domain wherever a public animal benchmark exists. PanAf500 (great-ape natural behavior, in-the-wild video, 500 clips with official splits and nine behavior classes) is the public animal benchmark we could identify, and we pre-registered its budget-shrinkage test (PSD-PANAF-PREREG-001, frozen before any run) after the E9b/c gradient was observable but before the PanAf run executed. Its full-budget reference (60.0%) lies below the 70% falsifier threshold of the gradient's strong form, so this run is a directional consistency check rather than a potential falsifier: the observed retention (88.7% at the pre-registered ten seeds; 85.8% at the thirty-seed amendment below; both in the PARTIAL band) is consistent with the high band predicted for separable references. Skeletons are model-extracted: a YOLO11xpose model fine-tuned on the ape subset of AP-10K (17-keypoint topology mapped onto the 24-slot layout with seven dead slots) infers keypoints on bbox-cropped frames (82.1% mean frame detection, 0.59 mean confidence; extraction noise enters all arms equally). 6. Conclusion and limitations
6.1. Conclusion We presented PSD, a physics-semantics decoupled framework for skeleton-based behavior recognition under
evolving evaluation criteria: a label-free physics layer provides self-supervised dynamics representations and unsupervised behavior proposals, while a lightweight semantic layer grows rule-engine seeds toward full taxonomy coverage through anchor-guided clustering, single-round confidence-filtered pseudo-labeling, and warm-started small-budget initialization. Experiments across the two exercised tiers and four public benchmarks spanning two kingdoms (NTU60, NTU120, UCF101, PanAf500) showed that pretraining yields discriminable dynamics representations (2.51× random baseline; a subject-identification probe, not a behavior measure), that the semantic layer attains 82.0% top-1 on 22 classes from a 20-clip budget via warm-started initialization (synthetic-offset tier, vs. 96.6% full-budget; the 20 clips are the marginal budget atop a fully labeled source domain; ± 4.3 across three seeds), and that synthetictier taxonomy transitions are absorbed at  3× lower wall-clock retraining cost at matched accuracy (measured 6.07×; accuracy differences inside the pre-registered < 2.3 pp noise band). Real-domain replications return FAIL at MLP scale and PARTIAL at backbone scale. An implementation-equivalence verification on NTU60 (three-stream

6.2. Limitations Thirteen limitations bound our claims.
L1: Proxy-caliber evidence for representation quality. Pretraining evidence uses subject identity as a -NN probe on InterPet4D (representation discriminability, not behavior accuracy); behavior-level evidence rests on the downstream experiments of Sections 4-5 (segmentation IoU 0.458± 0.049 under seeds pseudo-ground truth, pool precision 0.691± 0.013, the four-class public-real fine-tuning result below, and the cross-domain retention of E9), each with its caliber disclosed inline.
L2: Single species family. Public-real real-data validation covers canids only; other real-data tiers are validated in their own domains (PanAf500 in-domain, E9d), and no cross-family pretext transfer (canidfelid/equid/primate) has been tested; the NTU human-domain verification is not animal cross-family evidence. Sex- and gender-based analyses were not performed (see the Ethics statement); no dataset in this study supports them.
L3: Survey boundary on firstness. Our novelty claim rests on repository-scale search with zero occupancy; an arXiv/CrossRef re-verification at submission time surfaced point-supervised skeleton segmentation as the nearest neighbor (differentiated in Section 2), and we cannot exclude unpublished or non-indexed concurrent work.

L4: Scale of compute. All experiments ran on a single consumer GPU (RTX 5060 Laptop 8 GB); scaling behavior to multi-GPU pretraining and web-scale unlabeled pools is unverified.
L5: No advantage of uncertainty sampling (reproduced across three independent runs). In our short-budget active-learning studies (synthetic tier), entropy sampling did not outperform random selection under either weak or strong scorers: with cold-start scorers, random led by 7.9 pp at budget 100 (2 of 3 seeds) and 7.1 pp at 200 (3 of 3 seeds); a full-budget cold-start rerun reproduces the direction (random ahead by 12.3 pp at budget 100 and 7.8 pp at 200); with warm-started in-domain fine-tuned scorers, random again led by 4.2-5.0 pp across all three budget points. The synthetic-trained checkpoint additionally scored the public-real candidate pool with fully saturated softmax (mean top1-top2 logit margin 100.9 vs. 10.8 within the synthetic validation domain), degrading uncertainty signals to numerical zero. We therefore report uncertainty sampling strictly as an exploratory negative finding and make no sampling-efficiency claims.
L6: Structural constraints of the public-real tier. Under the pre-registered partial-class protocol, only 4 of 12 target classes pass the canine sample-size gate under the relaxed rule (3 under the strict rule; down/stand/scale have zero coverage), and the released pose-estimation subset holds 4.6 frames per video (28,197 images across 6,160 video directories; the Animal Kingdom paper reports 33,099 pose frames; a counting difference between the official figure and our local extraction, disclosed), with no canine-overlapping video reaching the  =30 length required by skeleton pipelines; a self-extraction pipeline (YOLO11-pose fine-tuned on dog-pose, then skeleton extraction and ST-GCN head fine-tuning on the 4-class subset) was adopted as mitigation, and its first-round outcome is quantified in L7.
L7: Severe class imbalance dominates the public-real result. The self-extraction round-one model reaches 44.9% overall validation accuracy on the four-class subset (seed 42; three-seed mean 41.5% ± 5.9 pp; 1.80× the 25% random baseline) under heavily skewed support (full list: watch 72 / track 46 / stay 27 / jump 27; train split 57/29/19/18); per-class accuracies collapse to watch 100% / track 23.5% / jump 0% / stay 0%; majority-class recall accounts for essentially all of the aggregate. We present this number with its per-class breakdown attached, restrict its interpretation to the four-class partial caliber, and treat aggregate-only reporting as prohibited for this tier.
L8: Real-domain skeletons carry 20/24 valid supervision channels. The dog-pose annotations never label four of the 24 graph joints (both eyes, withers, throat; the last four slots are absent from the source data), so every realdomain keypoint product in this paper is topology-isomorphic rather than fully supervised: the four dead channels are hard-masked at assembly and NaN-guarded downstream. Any real-domain skeleton-based number therefore rests on 20-channel effective supervision, and rules that depend on the withers landmark are structurally degraded in the pixel domain.
L9: Boundary conditions of cross-domain unsupervised enhancement (pre-registered negative at this resource level). The canine-tier failure consolidated in Section 4.4 is the paper's principal boundary condition: endto-end accuracy at a 13% budget is near chance (9.8% vs. 11.1%), below the generic-SSL control (15.2%), two of four partial-protocol classes are never recognized, and three pre-registered rescue levers returned null. The mechanism reading is a compound domain shift (clip-level label granularity misaligned with frame-accurate skeletons, with pool precision 0.11; mocap-to-wild pretext-target shift; and long-tailed class structure), documented in Section 4.4; it bounds every low-resource claim in this paper to the tiers measured. We also tested whether unlabeled cross-domain geometry transfers into the working-dog 2D pose domain: a pre-registered round augmented the frozen backbone with AdaBN-style (Li et al., 2017) statistics from 1,069 clips (570 pixel-domain, 499 APTv2) under a kinematic plausibility gate calibrated on 51 mocap sequences, then repeated the identical four-class head re-training protocol. The primary endpoint failed: 40.82% vs. 44.90% (-4.08 pp against a bit-identical same-day baseline rerun; paired three-seed deltas -4.08/+6.12/-8.16 pp, mean -2.04 pp inside the ± 5.9 pp seed-noise band). Because the adaptation demonstrably penetrated the backbone (85/85 BatchNorm statistics moved; the track profile swung 23.5%76.5% in the strongest single-source arm), the bottleneck is conversion, not supply: absent a label-alignment force, no adapted arm yields a legitimate aggregate gain; the only same-seed aggregate rise (+4.08 pp, APTv2-only arm) is a class-level swap (track tripled while watch fell 100%26.7%), a secondary signal only. The treadmill-only reference set contains no static behavior, so its low-speed band encodes reference bias rather than physics. We claim no accuracy benefit from unlabeled cross-domain enhancement at this caliber and identify label-in-the-loop alignment, not more unlabeled volume, as the missing fuel for any future flywheel claim, a diagnosis the corrected E7/E7b arms quantify on the canine tier.
