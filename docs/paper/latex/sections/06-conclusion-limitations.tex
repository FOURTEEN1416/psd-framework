\section{Conclusion and limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Conclusion}

We presented PSD, a physics--semantics decoupled framework for skeleton-based behavior recognition under evolving evaluation criteria: a label-free physics layer provides self-supervised dynamics representations and unsupervised behavior proposals, while a lightweight semantic layer grows rule-engine seeds toward full taxonomy coverage through anchor-guided clustering, single-round confidence-filtered pseudo-labeling, and warm-started small-budget initialization. Experiments across the two exercised tiers and four public benchmarks spanning two kingdoms (NTU60, NTU120, UCF101, PanAf500) showed that pretraining yields discriminable dynamics representations ($2.51\times$ random baseline; a subject-identification probe, not a behavior measure), that the semantic layer attains \textbf{82.0\% top-1 on 22 classes from a 20-clip budget via warm-started initialization} (synthetic-offset tier, vs.\ 96.6\% full-budget; the 20 clips are the marginal budget atop a fully labeled source domain; $\pm$ 4.3 across three seeds), and that synthetic-tier taxonomy transitions are absorbed at \textbf{$\geq 3\times$ lower wall-clock retraining cost} at matched accuracy (measured $6.07\times$; accuracy differences inside the pre-registered $<2.3$\,pp noise band), with real-domain replications returning FAIL at MLP scale and PARTIAL at backbone scale. An implementation-equivalence verification on NTU60 (three-stream fusion 77.97\% against a pre-registered acceptance line of 77.18\%) shows the re-implemented pipeline meets its equivalence criterion. Under the corrected pseudo-label protocol the end-to-end pipeline retains 90.7\% of full-budget accuracy at 10\% of the labels on NTU60 (NTU120 75.0\% under the pre-registered label-free pretext and 88.9\% under a disclosed interim supervised encoder; PanAf500 85.8\% at thirty seeds, PARTIAL; UCF101 at 66.6\% marks the collapse boundary), with the pseudo-label iteration adding $+1.48$\,pp; separately, a linear head on frozen NTU60 features alone retains 88.7\%. Under an identical budget protocol on NTU60 (Table~\ref{tab:h2h}), the 10\% semantic arm (67.53\%) also edges re-implemented AimCLR linear evaluation (66.74\% $\pm$ 0.05 over five initialization seeds; Welch $p{=}1.1\times10^{-6}$) and an end-to-end supervised ST-GCN (64.84\% $\pm$ 2.18; the gap is not significant under Welch due to that arm's seed instability), the ordering holds on two independent subset redraws, while full-budget supervised training dominates (83.46\% against the 74.45\% frozen-feature reference)---the decoupled advantage is specific to the scarce-label regime. At a 13\% budget on the canine tier accuracy falls \emph{below} chance (9.8\% vs.\ 11.1\%, with a generic-SSL control at 15.2\%)---a tier-dependent boundary that bounds the low-resource claim to the regimes measured. The contribution is the boundary and the protocol that establishes it, not a universal recognizer: decoupling---frozen physics, revisable semantics---is worth deploying where a frozen backbone stays reusable and semantics are cheap to rebuild, and the same pre-registered protocol exposes where it is not. Multi-round pseudo-label iteration is not claimed as beneficial (first-to-last comparison n.s.\ after Holm correction, $p=0.090$), and the SMQ segmentation component is treated as an auxiliary proposal source (a dedicated downstream ablation attributes $\Delta = 0.0$ accuracy to it). The pre-registered attribution controls (Section~\ref{ssec:boundary}; Table~\ref{tab:attribution-controls}) show the $\geq 3\times$ saving is attributable to semantic rebuilding rather than freezing alone: on the synthetic tier, a frozen physics layer with a standard linear head collapses to 68.6\% top-1 against 98.2\% for full fine-tuning; on the three real E9-series tiers (frozen-encoder arms), the 10\% PSD arm retains 88.9\%/66.6\%/85.8\% of the full-budget reference (verdicts \textbf{PARTIAL}/\textbf{FAILS}/\textbf{PARTIAL}); and the merge/rename zero-retraining bound, re-measured on the TRANS-002 episode itself (reinforcement E1, same-day specified), reads 68.04\% (rename-oracle upper; argmax-vote lower 67.97\%) at the same 10\% labeled-head budget. On that transfer the decoupled rebuild (75.3\%) is bounded from above on accuracy by its equal-budget alternatives---the coupled arm (79.8\% at the frozen three-seed budget; 79.7\% over ten seeds) and the matched-solver control (82.6\% over ten seeds, Holm-corrected $p=0.004$)---while its margin over the zero-cost path is purchased with $\mathcal{Y}'$ labels. For transitions requiring new supervision (synthetic split/addition, E3), the zero-shot remap names none of the new classes (0\%), and the decoupled rebuild reaches 45.7\% overall with 10 new labels per class---$5\times$ fewer new labels and $3.0\times$ lower median wall-clock than full retraining (97.1\%). A labeled-budget ladder on the same episode (reinforcement E3b; Table~\ref{tab:kladder}; $k\in\{10,25,50,100\}$ fresh labels per new class; pool enlarged to 100 clips/class, so $k{=}10$ is a trend reference) shows the decoupled arm rising from 45.3\% to 72.4\% (three seeds 42--44 per rung) but saturating at the frozen-feature ceiling (frozen-head arm: 71.7\%) and never approaching the coupled arm (95.9\%): the margin stays between 23.45 and 50.57\,pp---far outside the $\pm2.3$\,pp parity band---so no critical $k^{\ast}$ exists within the tested range and the residual-value hypothesis is \emph{not supported at any tested budget}; decoupling's measured value in this regime is confined to label and wall-clock savings (at $k{=}10$: 100 vs.\ 1{,}000 new labels; 92\,s vs.\ 234\,s, seed 42), and closing the accuracy gap appears to require adapting the frozen encoder rather than adding labels.

\subsection{Limitations}

Thirteen limitations bound our claims.

\paragraph{L1: Proxy-caliber evidence for representation quality.}
Pretraining evidence uses subject identity as a $k$-NN probe on InterPet4D---representation discriminability, not behavior accuracy; behavior-level evidence rests on the downstream experiments of Sections~\ref{sec:experiments}--\ref{sec:ablation} (segmentation IoU 0.458$\pm$ 0.049 under seeds pseudo-ground truth, pool precision 0.691$\pm$ 0.013, the four-class public-real fine-tuning result below, and the cross-domain retention of E9), each with its caliber disclosed inline.

\paragraph{L2: Single species family.}
Public-real real-data validation covers canids only; other real-data tiers are validated in their own domains (PanAf500 in-domain, E9d), and no cross-family pretext transfer (canid$\to$felid/equid/primate) has been tested---the NTU human-domain verification is not animal cross-family evidence. Sex- and gender-based analyses were not performed (see the Ethics statement); no dataset in this study supports them.

\paragraph{L3: Survey boundary on firstness.}
Our novelty claim rests on repository-scale search with zero occupancy; an arXiv/CrossRef re-verification at submission time surfaced point-supervised skeleton segmentation as the nearest neighbor (differentiated in Section~\ref{sec:related}), and we cannot exclude unpublished or non-indexed concurrent work.

\paragraph{L4: Scale of compute.}
All experiments ran on a single consumer GPU (RTX 5060 Laptop 8\,GB); scaling behavior to multi-GPU pretraining and web-scale unlabeled pools is unverified.

\paragraph{L5: No advantage of uncertainty sampling---reproduced across three independent runs.}
In our short-budget active-learning studies (synthetic tier), entropy sampling did not outperform random selection under either weak or strong scorers: with cold-start scorers, random led by 7.9\,pp at budget 100 (2 of 3 seeds) and 7.1\,pp at 200 (3 of 3 seeds); a full-budget cold-start rerun reproduces the direction (random ahead by 12.3\,pp at budget 100 and 7.8\,pp at 200); with warm-started in-domain fine-tuned scorers, random again led by 4.2--5.0\,pp across all three budget points. The synthetic-trained checkpoint additionally scored the public-real candidate pool with fully saturated softmax (mean top1$-$top2 logit margin 100.9 vs.\ $\approx$10.8 within the synthetic validation domain), degrading uncertainty signals to numerical zero. We therefore report uncertainty sampling strictly as an exploratory negative finding and make no sampling-efficiency claims.

\paragraph{L6: Structural constraints of the public-real tier.}
Under the pre-registered partial-class protocol, only 4 of 12 target classes pass the canine sample-size gate under the relaxed rule (3 under the strict rule; down/stand/scale have zero coverage), and the released pose-estimation subset holds $\approx$4.6 frames per video (28{,}197 images across 6{,}160 video directories; the Animal Kingdom paper reports 33{,}099 pose frames---a counting difference between the official figure and our local extraction, disclosed), with no canine-overlapping video reaching the $T{=}30$ length required by skeleton pipelines; a self-extraction pipeline (YOLO11-pose fine-tuned on dog-pose, then skeleton extraction and ST-GCN head fine-tuning on the 4-class subset) was adopted as mitigation, and its first-round outcome is quantified in L7.

\paragraph{L7: Severe class imbalance dominates the public-real result.}
The self-extraction round-one model reaches 44.9\% overall validation accuracy on the four-class subset (seed 42; three-seed mean 41.5\% $\pm$ 5.9\,pp; $1.80\times$ the 25\% random baseline) under heavily skewed support (full list: watch 72 / track 46 / stay 27 / jump 27; train split 57/29/19/18); per-class accuracies collapse to watch 100\% / track 23.5\% / jump 0\% / stay 0\%---majority-class recall accounts for essentially all of the aggregate. We present this number with its per-class breakdown attached, restrict its interpretation to the four-class partial caliber, and treat aggregate-only reporting as prohibited for this tier.

\paragraph{L8: Real-domain skeletons carry 20/24 valid supervision channels.}
The dog-pose annotations never label four of the 24 graph joints (both eyes, withers, throat---the last four slots are absent from the source data), so every real-domain keypoint product in this paper is topology-isomorphic rather than fully supervised: the four dead channels are hard-masked at assembly and NaN-guarded downstream. Any real-domain skeleton-based number therefore rests on 20-channel effective supervision, and rules that depend on the withers landmark are structurally degraded in the pixel domain.

\paragraph{L9: Boundary conditions of cross-domain unsupervised enhancement---pre-registered negative at this resource level.}
The canine-tier failure consolidated in Section~\ref{ssec:boundary} is the paper's principal boundary condition: end-to-end accuracy at a 13\% budget is near chance (9.8\% vs.\ 11.1\%), below the generic-SSL control (15.2\%), two of four partial-protocol classes are never recognized, and three pre-registered rescue levers returned null. The mechanism reading is a compound domain shift---clip-level label granularity misaligned with frame-accurate skeletons (pool precision $\approx$0.11), mocap-to-wild pretext--target shift, and long-tailed class structure---documented in Section~\ref{ssec:boundary}; it bounds every low-resource claim in this paper to the tiers measured.
We also tested whether unlabeled cross-domain geometry transfers into the working-dog 2D pose domain: a pre-registered round augmented the frozen backbone with AdaBN-style~\citep{li2017revisiting} statistics from 1{,}069 clips (570 pixel-domain, 499 APTv2) under a kinematic plausibility gate calibrated on 51 mocap sequences, then repeated the identical four-class head re-training protocol. The primary endpoint failed: 40.82\% vs.\ 44.90\% ($-4.08$\,pp against a bit-identical same-day baseline rerun; paired three-seed deltas $-4.08$/$+6.12$/$-8.16$\,pp, mean $-2.04$\,pp inside the $\pm$ 5.9\,pp seed-noise band). Because the adaptation demonstrably penetrated the backbone (85/85 BatchNorm statistics moved; the track profile swung 23.5\%$\to$76.5\% in the strongest single-source arm), the bottleneck is conversion, not supply: absent a label-alignment force, no adapted arm yields a legitimate aggregate gain---the only same-seed aggregate rise ($+4.08$\,pp, APTv2-only arm) is a class-level swap (track tripled while watch fell 100\%$\to$26.7\%), a secondary signal only. The treadmill-only reference set contains no static behavior, so its low-speed band encodes reference bias rather than physics. We claim no accuracy benefit from unlabeled cross-domain enhancement at this caliber and identify label-in-the-loop alignment, not more unlabeled volume, as the missing fuel for any future flywheel claim---a diagnosis the corrected E7/E7b arms quantify on the canine tier.

\paragraph{L10: The pretraining-benefit crossover point is bracketed, not localized.}
A low-resource gradient ablation (synthetic tier; scratch vs.\ pretraining-initialized, two arms $\times$ three seeds, matched budgets) shows the fine-tuning benefit of self-supervised pretraining decaying with annotation budget to statistical equivalence (a disclosed $-2.3$\,pp reversal at 352 clips): $+21.2$\,pp at 88 clips, $+9.9$\,pp at 176, $-2.3$\,pp at 352, $+0.15$\,pp (n.s.) at 1760. The zero crossing lies between 10 and 20 clips per class---linear interpolation near 15--20---but no finer tiers were run, so its location remains unlocalized. Two caliber constraints apply: gradient tiers ran on CPU, the saturation tier on GPU, so cross-tier absolute accuracies are not poolable (trends rest on within-tier paired deltas); and the lowest tier validates on only 22 samples (one sample = 4.55\,pp), so effect sizes there are directional. The curve is presented whole; no claim extends outside the measured tiers.

\paragraph{L11: E9's retention cannot separate filtering from pool size.}
At NTU scale the confidence gate accepts $\approx$98\% of the unlabeled pool, so the 90.7\% retention characterizes the pipeline's budget behavior under pseudo-label restoration, not the contribution of filtering per se; the 10\% labeled subset is a single stratified draw, so the reported spread covers self-training seeds but not subset resampling; and the precision-drop stopping rule is disabled on this tier (no rule-derived consensus reference exists), with pool precision against the official labels ($\approx$0.69) reported only as a post-hoc diagnostic outside the control path. The external-SSL comparison is likewise harness-limited: the Mean-Teacher baseline runs on the same frozen features with a confidence threshold ($\tau{=}0.95$) whose selectivity is the opposite of the pipeline's near-full pool acceptance, so the $+20.7$\,pp gap measures the value of near-full pool restoration over selective pseudo-labeling, not the superiority of pseudo-label restoration over SSL in general---an end-to-end SSL comparison (video-level, learned representations) remains outside this study's scope. A post-hoc $2\times2$ decomposition (PSD-POOLDECOMP-PREREG-001, registered after the series; four arms differing only in pool composition---gate on/off $\times$ full pool/random equal-size subset, ten seeds each) measured both factors directly: the filtering main effect is $-0.02$\,pp and the pool-size main effect is $0.0$\,pp (all four arms within $67.53$--$67.58\%$), so at this scale the pipeline's $+1.48$\,pp gain is attributable to pseudo-label self-training over a near-full pool---the gate is inert at this operating point (load-bearing only in the canine tier's selective regime, where it filters on calibrated margins).

\paragraph{L12: The $\geq 3\times$ transition-cost bound.}
The bound is measured on the synthetic tier (E6, $6.07\times$; three seeds 42--44, per-seed paired ratios in the supplementary material) and replicated on the human benchmark at backbone scale (PSD-NTU-TRANS-002, frozen before the run, seeds 42--44): at the full budget the median coupled/decoupled wall-clock ratio is 15.8$\times$ (9897\,s vs 626\,s; a final-segment-only bound of 6.2$\times$ still clears the $3\times$ line) with a coupled-over-decoupled accuracy margin of 4.5\,pp (outside the $\pm$ 2.3\,pp band), and at the 10\% budget the ratio reaches 32.5$\times$ with a +2.6\,pp decoupled advantage (PARTIAL at both budgets). Two attribution gaps bound this claim. First, the ratio is available to any frozen-backbone design; the pre-registered frozen-backbone control (Section~\ref{ssec:boundary}; Table~\ref{tab:attribution-controls}) quantifies the gap---naive freezing reaches only 68.6\% on the synthetic tier (vs.\ 98.2\% full fine-tuning) and retains 88.9\%/66.6\%/85.8\% on NTU120/UCF101/PanAf500 under the interim supervised encoder (PARTIAL/FAILS/PARTIAL; the pre-registered label-free pretext reads 75.0\% on NTU120, FAILS---Section~\ref{sec:comparison})---so the $\geq 3\times$ bound at matched accuracy is attributable to the semantic-layer reconstruction, not to freezing alone. Second, the exercised transitions are remap and merge types; for pure merges and renames a zero-retraining upper bound applies, and rebuild-based savings must be read against it---the bound reads 76.8\%/76.5\% (rename-oracle upper/hard-vote lower) on the $60\to10$ coarse-grouping episode and 68.04\%/67.97\% on the TRANS-002 $60\to49$ episode itself (reinforcement E1; same 10\% labeled head budget, zero retraining)---two calibers that are not interchangeable. On the backbone-scale replication the accuracy ordering is matched-solver 82.6\% $>$ coupled 79.7\% (ten seeds; Holm-corrected $p=0.004$) $>$ decoupled rebuild 75.3\% $>$ same-episode bound 68.04\%: the rebuild is dominated on accuracy by equal-budget alternatives while its saving is wall-clock only. The residual-value question is probed on transitions requiring new supervision (E3/E3b; Table~\ref{tab:kladder}): the split/addition ladder runs 0\% (zero-shot remap) $\to$ 45.7\% (decoupled rebuild, $k{=}10$) $\to$ 68.0\% (frozen head) $\to$ 97.1\% (coupled), and the $k$-ladder rises 45.3\%$\to$72.4\% with the coupled-minus-decoupled margin never leaving 23.45--50.57\,pp---no critical $k^{\ast}$ exists within the tested range, so the residual-value hypothesis is \emph{not supported at any tested budget}; decoupling's measured value in this regime is the label and wall-clock saving (at $k{=}10$: 100 vs.\ 1{,}000 new labels; 92\,s vs.\ 234\,s, seed 42), and closing the accuracy gap appears to require adapting the frozen encoder rather than adding labels. We did not measure a real-data taxonomy transition on working-dog data because no public canine dataset provides two aligned taxonomies over the same unlabeled stream; the bound's magnitude on real working-dog data is an open question the pre-registered K9 pilot addresses. The MLP-scale TRANS-001 failure is re-read as scale-bound (no cost to save at 35\,s); the $\geq 3\times$ claim is stated as a synthetic-tier measurement whose real-domain replication returns \textbf{PARTIAL} at both budgets, not as a domain-universal guarantee.

\paragraph{L13: Head-to-head evidence is confined to one benchmark and re-implemented baselines.}
The published-method comparison (Table~\ref{tab:h2h}) is deliberately narrow: one benchmark (NTU60 xsub), two baselines run by us under the identical budget protocol---the re-implemented AimCLR linear-evaluation arm (in-house pretext weights; the official released checkpoint was unreachable, with fidelity anchored by the equivalence check of Section~\ref{sec:comparison}) and a reduced-capacity supervised ST-GCN (16 hidden channels, 0.84M parameters---the published full-capacity model is not reproduced, so its 83.46\% full-budget accuracy is a same-harness reference). At the 10\% budget the semantic arm (67.53\% $\pm$ 0.24) edges both (66.74\% $\pm$ 0.05 / 64.84\% $\pm$ 2.18 over five initialization seeds each; the AimCLR gap is significance-tested at Welch $p{=}1.1\times10^{-6}$, the ST-GCN gap is not significant under Welch due to that arm's seed instability, Mann--Whitney $p{=}6.7\times10^{-4}$), the ordering is preserved on two independent subset redraws (margins $+7.3$ to $+7.8$\,pp over AimCLR), and at full budget end-to-end supervision dominates (83.46\% vs.\ 74.30\%/74.45\%)---no general superiority over published methods is claimed; the 10\% edge is specific to the scarce-label regime, is significance-tested only against re-implemented baselines under our harness (post-hoc two-test family; Section~\ref{sec:comparison}), and its magnitude is subset-draw-dependent. The remaining baselines stay internal controls (random/majority/scratch references; the self-implemented Mean-Teacher of L11); no comparison against published \emph{reported} numbers is claimed (different datasets, label spaces, extraction pipelines), the scope statements of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} register the AimCLR++ backbone and the $\tau$/K sweeps as out of scope, and an end-to-end comparison against published animal-recognition systems remains outside this study.
